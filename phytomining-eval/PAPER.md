# One sentence, two densities: an evaluation of *Quantum Biofortification and In-Situ Phytomining*

karagos01, October 2026

## Abstract

M. Mazgal, *Quantum Biofortification and In-Situ Phytomining: Design of an
Autonomous Closed-Loop Nanometallic Agricultural Matrix* (Zenodo, 6 October
2026, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23174932](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23174932))
proposes burying 25 t/ha of biochar-and-bentonite pellets carrying extremophilic
bacteria, 5 kg of gold over 30 ha, and a profile of "stable isotopes with a
non-zero nuclear spin", so that crops and the people and livestock eating them
gain "absolute structural immunity" and "the theoretical blockade of natural
biological aging processes".

Four measurements. §4 states a volumetric density, a count per square metre and
a mass per hectare in one sentence as if they agreed; at the stated plough depth
the first is **24 times** the second, and the paper's entire costed design rests
on the second. §3.3 calls ¹³⁸La a stable isotope: it is a **primordial
radionuclide**, half-life 1.03·10¹¹ years, decaying 65.5 % β⁺ and 34.5 % β⁻. The
premise of §3.3 fails for lanthanum before that even matters, because ¹³⁹La —
99.91 % of all lanthanum — **already has spin 7/2**, so there is nothing to
select for. And at natural isotopic composition the matrix raises the field's
lanthanum by **about a fifth** of what is already there while leaving the ¹³⁸La
fraction exactly where it was, which makes the "most radical innovation of the
matrix" an addition of ordinary rock.

The paper's own reference list inverts three of its load-bearing claims. The
cited author of the magnetic isotope effect measures **3–5× suppression** of DNA
synthesis for exactly the three isotopes the paper cites for acceleration, and
proposes them as a cytostatic; the cited gold paper has gold **inducing**
oxidative stress and being exported by the cell, where the paper has biogenic
gold "entirely blocking" it; and the cited phytomining review is about
**removing** metal from soil and smelting it. Those three are set out with their
verbatim evidence in the companion
[`reference-audit`](../reference-audit) repository and
are summarised in §6 here.

Two findings go the other way. The §1 premise — 30–50 % of applied nitrogen
recovered by the first crop — is correct. And the added ¹³⁸La is radiologically
negligible, four to five orders of magnitude below the potassium-40 already in
the soil, so calling it stable is an error about the nuclide and not a safety
problem with the proposal.

## 1. What is correct

**The opening premise checks out.** §1 says a plant assimilates "only 30% to
50% of the supplied nutrients in real-time". Fertilizer nitrogen recovery by the
first crop is 30–50 %, so the paper starts from a real and well-quantified
problem, and the leaching it describes is the standard reason for it.

**Biochar as a carrier for a bacterial inoculant is a real precedent**, not an
invention of this paper, and the cited Lehmann review supports it in its own
abstract — "as inoculant carriers". The physical concept of a cold-pressed,
delayed-release pellet with spores in its pores is ordinary materials
engineering and nothing here argues against it.

**The nuclear spins are all stated correctly.** ¹³⁸La has I = 5, ²⁵Mg and ⁶⁷Zn
have I = 5/2, ⁴³Ca has I = 7/2. They are non-zero for the right reason, and the
contrast the paper draws on — that the abundant isotopes of magnesium, zinc and
calcium are even-even and so have I = 0 exactly — is sound. The magnetic isotope
effect it invokes is real physics with a real literature.

**The hypercomplex vocabulary is absent.** Counted over the extracted text,
"Maxwell", "octonion", "quaternion", "hypercomplex", "non-associative",
"tensor" and "causal" all occur zero times. This deposit is one of six of the
author's sixteen with none of it; the census is in
[`reference-audit`](../reference-audit).

## 2. One sentence states two densities 24 times apart

§4, verbatim:

> "Densification to the level of 1 pellet per 5 cm³ of soil (at a plowing depth
> of 30 cm) equates to approximately 2,500 pellets per 1 m², or 25 tons of
> matrix per 1 hectare."

At 30 cm depth there are 300 000 cm³ of soil under a square metre. One pellet
per 5 cm³ is therefore **60 000 pellets/m²**, not 2 500. Put the other way,
2 500 pellets/m² is one pellet per **120 cm³**.

Which of the two does the paper use? The mass figure decides it: 25 t/ha is
2.5 kg/m², which at 2 500 pellets/m² is exactly **1 g per pellet** — a sensible
pellet. At 60 000/m² it would be 0.042 g. So the mass budget, the 750-tonne
material requirement and the $1 000 000 case study are all built on the 2 500,
and the "5 cm³" is the figure that does not fit.

That matters because the 5 cm³ is not decoration. The sentence immediately
before it is the justification: the root "must not 'search' for distant deposits
via fungi; it must be in constant physical contact with the nanomatrix." Taking
that requirement at face value, at the paper's own 1 g pellet:

| | honouring 1 per 5 cm³ | the paper's figure |
|---|---|---|
| matrix | **600 t/ha** | 25 t/ha |
| for 30 ha | **18 000 t** | 750 t |
| at the same $/t | **$24 000 000** | $1 000 000 |

So the physical-contact requirement and the costed design are not the same
design, and they are a factor of 24 apart. This is one arithmetic slip in one
sentence rather than a defect in the concept — but it is the sentence that
connects the biological motivation to the economics, which is the join the whole
case study hangs on.

Neither density is contact, incidentally. On a cubic lattice 2 500/m² over
0.3 m gives a mean spacing of 4.93 cm, so a root is never more than 2.5 cm from
a pellet; at 60 000/m² it is 1.71 cm and 0.9 cm. Both are distances a root has
to grow across, and the paper gives no root-length density to compare them
against.

## 3. ¹³⁸La is not a stable isotope

The abstract says "a precisely defined profile of **stable isotopes** (including
elements with non-zero nuclear spin such as 138La, 43Ca, and 25Mg)". §3.3 says
"the selection of rare, **stable** isotopes". The Zenodo keywords include
"Stable Isotopes".

¹³⁸La is a **primordial radionuclide**: half-life 1.03(1)·10¹¹ years, decaying
β⁺ 65.5 % to ¹³⁸Ba and β⁻ 34.5 % to ¹³⁸Ce, natural abundance 8.881(71)·10⁻⁴. It
is one of a handful of odd-odd nuclides that survive from the formation of the
Earth, which is precisely why it is rare. The rarity the paper selects for and
the instability it overlooks are the same fact about the same nuclide.

This is a hard, checkable error, and it is in the abstract, the body and the
metadata. It is not, however, a safety problem — see §7.

## 4. The premise fails for lanthanum before stability matters

§3.3 calls the "Quantum Circuit" its most radical innovation, and the selection
rule is "rare, stable isotopes with a non-zero (and often asymmetric) nuclear
spin".

**¹³⁹La, which is 99.91 % of all lanthanum, has spin 7/2.** Non-zero, and larger
than nothing. So for lanthanum there is no spin to be gained by selecting the
rare isotope — the common one already has one.

For magnesium, zinc and calcium the paper's logic does work: ²⁴Mg, ²⁶Mg,
⁶⁴/⁶⁶/⁶⁸/⁷⁰Zn and ⁴⁰Ca are even-even and have I = 0 exactly, so there the choice
of the odd isotope genuinely changes the magnetic environment, and that is the
basis of the real magnetic isotope effect. Lanthanum is the one element in the
list for which the rule has nothing to select, and it is the element the section
is built around — the ¹³⁸La "magnetic gyroscope" in the synapse is the paper's
single most specific mechanistic claim.

## 5. At natural abundance the matrix adds less than the field already holds

The paper never says the isotopes are enriched, and its budget — 1 t of
lanthanum oxide plus 3 t of trace elements inside a $146 000 line — is
consistent with ordinary commercial material. Take it at its word and compute
what the matrix changes.

One tonne of La₂O₃ is 853 kg of lanthanum, which over 30 ha is 2.84 g/m². The
ploughed layer already contains lanthanum at the upper-crustal abundance of
31 ppm:

| bulk density | soil under 1 m² | La already present | the matrix adds | new concentration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 g/cm³ | 330 kg | 10.23 g/m² | +27.8 % | 39.6 ppm |
| 1.4 g/cm³ | 420 kg | 13.02 g/m² | +21.8 % | 37.8 ppm |
| 1.6 g/cm³ | 480 kg | 14.88 g/m² | +19.1 % | 36.9 ppm |

So the most radical innovation raises the field's lanthanum by roughly a fifth —
and, at natural isotopic composition, leaves the ¹³⁸La fraction exactly where it
was, at 0.0888 %. **A natural isotope ratio is not something a soil can be
deficient in.**

The same holds more strongly for the other two. ²⁵Mg is 10.00 % of all
magnesium, and magnesium is a major soil element: across a swept range of
0.2–3 % by mass the ploughed layer already holds **84 to 1260 g/m² of ²⁵Mg**.
⁶⁷Zn is 4.10 % of all zinc, giving 0.34–2.07 g/m² already present. Chlorophyll
already contains ²⁵Mg at 10.00 % of its magnesium and zinc-finger proteins
already contain ⁶⁷Zn at 4.10 % of their zinc. Whatever size the magnetic isotope
effect has, it is already running in that field, and the matrix does not move
the ratio.

The alternative reading is not available either. To obtain one tonne of ¹³⁸La at
its natural abundance would require about **1 126 tonnes** of natural lanthanum
as feed, before any separation cost. Unenriched, the Quantum Circuit adds
nothing; enriched, the budget is wrong by orders of magnitude. The paper needs
one of the two readings and has costed neither.

## 6. The references invert three of the load-bearing claims

All six of this deposit's references were read for content in the companion
[`reference-audit`](../reference-audit) repository,
which holds the verbatim evidence and records how far each work could be read.
Three results bear directly on the claims above.

**Buchachenko, on the magnetic isotope effect.** The paper cites him for ²⁵Mg
and ⁶⁷Zn that "accelerate the chemical kinetics of DNA transcription and ATP
synthesis". His own result for exactly that trio:

> "The substitution of these catalyzing ions in polymerases by the ions ²⁵Mg²⁺,
> ⁴³Ca²⁺, and ⁶⁷Zn²⁺ with magnetic nuclei was shown to produce a huge isotope
> effects: nuclear magnetic ions **by 3–5 times suppress DNA synthesis**"

> "A new **anti-cancer strategy** is suggested based on the using of the nuclear
> magnetic ions of magnesium, calcium, and zinc as a powerful and universal
> means to **selectively kill** only cancer cells."

Repair is also 1.5× slower. Only the ATP claim survives — and only for ²⁵Mg at
99.9 % enrichment delivered by an injectable nanoparticle, and it failed
replication in PNAS in 2012, which the paper does not cite. The ⁶⁷Zn half of
the gloss is additionally an anachronism: Buchachenko's biological zinc work is
from 2013, four years after the 2009 book cited.

**Reith et al., on gold.** The paper has biogenic gold nanoparticles as
"ultimate scavengers of reactive oxygen species (ROS), entirely blocking
cellular corrosion". The cited work: "Au detoxification is mediated by a
combination of **efflux**, reduction, and possibly methylation", and "one major
toxic effect of Au(III) … is the **induction of oxidative stress**", with a
minimum inhibitory concentration of 2 µM. The cell exports gold to survive it,
and gold induces the species the paper has it scavenging. The citation also does
not resolve as printed: the real paper is Reith et al. (2009), PNAS 106(42),
17757–17762.

**Sheoran et al., on phytomining.** Phytomining in that review is "a … technology
of **phytoremediation**" in which the crop is harvested as "bio-ore" and
"smelted, and recovered" — metal is **removed** from soil and sold. The paper
cites it as the basis for **delivering** gold, lanthanum and vanadium into edible
biomass and keeping them in the system. Opposite direction of material flow and
opposite purpose.

Two further mismatches are smaller. The paper says biochar "provides massive
cation exchange capacity"; Lehmann says biochars "typically have **initially
low** cation exchange capacities (CEC)" and that higher pyrolysis temperature
lowers it further. And Wright & Upadhyaya is glossed for the "mechanics of
glomalin production and its role in … water retention", but it is a survey of 37
samples whose single output is a regression, water appears in it only as the
destructive agent in wet sieving, and the paper's body never mentions glomalin
or mycorrhiza except to bypass them.

## 7. The economics, and the one thing that is reassuring

**The gold line is stale rather than wrong in method.** $417 000 for 5 kg
implies $83.40/g, which is $2 594/troy oz. Spot on the day of deposit was
$4 168.80/oz, or $134.03/g, so 5 kg cost $670 150 — the line is short by
$253 150 and the headline total becomes $1 253 150.

**The return needs a perpetual discount rate at or below 2.5 %.** Against
$25 000/yr of claimed savings, $1 000 000 has a simple payback of **40 years**,
and the present value of the saving in perpetuity equals the capital at exactly
2.50 % per year. Above that the net present value is negative. The paper calls
this "permanent capital", which is the right frame for the argument — but the
frame then requires the inventory to actually be permanent.

**The closed loop needs annual losses under 1.72 %.** For more than half the
metal inventory to survive to the 40-year payback, total annual leakage has to
stay below that figure, covering harvest removal net of returned manure,
leaching, erosion and runoff at once. The paper assumes the return fraction is
exactly 1 and gives no figure for any component of it. Phytomining works by
concentrating metal into biomass, and biomass leaves the field, so this is the
mechanism of the paper working against the premise of its own loop.

**And the radiology is fine, which is worth stating plainly.** One tonne of
natural La₂O₃ contains 757 g of ¹³⁸La, a total of 705 kBq spread over 30 ha, or
2.35 Bq/m². The potassium-40 already in that ploughed layer is 42 000–294 000
Bq/m². The addition is **four to five orders of magnitude** below the natural
background at every point of the range. Calling ¹³⁸La stable is an error about
the nuclide, not a hazard in the proposal, and those are different things.

## 8. What cannot be measured here

§3.1–3.3 and §5 make claims that are not of a form arithmetic can reach:
"absolute structural immunity", "entirely blocking cellular corrosion",
"immediate repair of DNA strand breaks", synapses that "theoretically exhibit
quantum coherence and extreme acceleration of cognitive transmission", a
community "impervious to degenerative cellular diseases", and "the theoretical
blockade of natural biological aging processes".

These are not measured here and no attempt is made to. Two observations about
their form, which is all that is available. None is stated with a dose, a
duration, an endpoint or a comparison group, so none is falsifiable as written.
And the nearest thing to a mechanism — the ¹³⁸La synapse — has no carrier in the
reference list at all: the paper's only lanthanide citation contains the words
neuron, synapse, brain, nuclear spin, quantum, coherence and aging zero times
each, and it specifically doubts the calcium substitution the mechanism needs
("REs are not perfect mimics of Ca … may not play a general role as Ca
substitutes in Ca-dependent proteins").

This deposit is also the point at which the series begins proposing
interventions on people rather than on hardware — biomass grown on this matrix
is to be eaten by "animals and humans" for generations. That is a change in kind
from the other deposits and is recorded as such, without further comment.

## 9. Limitations

No soil was amended, no crop was grown and no isotope was measured. Everything
here is arithmetic on the preprint's own parameters and on published constants,
because the deposit ships no code, no measurements and no figures.

Provenance is tagged in `constants.py`. `[P]` marks the preprint's own values,
`[V]` values verified against a named source while this was written, and `[A]`
**assumed ranges that are swept rather than asserted** — bulk density, soil
magnesium and zinc, crustal gold, soil potassium-40, and the discount and loss
rates. Every conclusion drawn from an `[A]` value is computed across its whole
range and stated only where it holds throughout. That tagging exists because the
reference audit found that citing a plausible figure from memory is exactly how a
bibliography goes wrong; two constants used in an earlier draft of this work
were demoted to `[A]` when they could not be checked against a primary source
here.

The crustal lanthanum figure is the one external value that carries a
conclusion, and it is `[V]`: 31 ppm, Rudnick & Gao, *Composition of the
Continental Crust*. The soil magnesium and zinc contents are `[A]`, so the
statement they support is the weaker one that survives the whole range.

The spacing figures in §2 assume a cubic lattice and so are a best case; real
broadcast-and-plough distribution is less uniform, which makes the mean distance
to the nearest pellet larger, not smaller.

## 10. Data and code availability

`constants.py` holds every constant with its provenance tag and source, and no

reproduce §§2–5 and §7. `run_all.sh` runs everything offline in about a second;
`results.log` is the output of one run. The reference verdicts are not duplicated
